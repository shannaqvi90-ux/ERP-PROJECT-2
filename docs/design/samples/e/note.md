# Direction E: "Financial clarity"

An editorial, typographic direction in the manner of the best financial pages: warm paper, ink, hairline rules and heavy section rules, serif hero figures, and grids that carry their own small charts. Colour is held back so that type, weight and alignment carry the meaning. A masthead replaces the left rail: both wordmarks, a dateline, search, the density, theme and language switches, and a section bar for the modules.

**Palette.** Paper #FAF8F3 ground, white for panes and fields, ink #1A1820 for text and for the heavy rules, warm greys for secondary text (#57534C, #6E6960, both AA on paper) and hairlines (#DDD7CA). Violet #6A00E8 (light) / #9B5CFF (dark) is used only for the one primary action, the selected row and tab, and focus. Red #B42318 is used only for negatives, overdue amounts and breaches; there are no tinted status pills. Dark is a warm charcoal (#141311, panes #1B1A17) with off-white ink, an editorial night rather than neon. High contrast is black with white rules, yellow focus and selection edge, and visible bar tracks.

**Type.** English: Schibsted Grotesk (variable, SIL OFL) for the interface, a news grotesque that stays legible at 11 to 13 px; Source Serif 4 (variable with optical sizes, SIL OFL) for titles and for every amount, so figures read like a printed statement, with tabular lining digits and narrow punctuation. Arabic: IBM Plex Sans Arabic (SIL OFL) for the interface and Noto Naskh Arabic (SIL OFL) for titles; Arabic sizes are one step larger, body leading 1.7, headings 1.5, no letter-spacing. Hierarchy of amounts: hero figures in the serif at 36 to 50 px, key figures at 26 px, grid amounts at 13.5 px; the decimal part is lighter and the currency code is small, bold and letter-spaced beside every amount. Totals carry a single rule above and a double rule below, as accountants draw them. KWD, BHD and OMR show three decimals.

**Spacing and shape.** 4 px grid, 40 px page margins. Comfortable is the default (36 px rows, 34 px controls) because the direction is made for reading figures; Compact (28 px rows) is one click away in the masthead. Radius 2 to 4 px; shadows only on the save bar and menus. Separation comes from rules: 2 px ink for sections, 1 px hairlines for rows, dotted leaders in briefing lists.

**Components.** Masthead with dateline and section bar; daily briefing with four numbered action columns (overdue, approvals, stock, credit) and one primary action, key figures with sparklines and deltas, a ledger-style activity table and a dashed empty-state card that leads to the first step; list header with headline figures and view tabs with counts; grid with credit-used bars marked at the limit, ageing stacked bars, 12-month trend lines (mirrored in Arabic so time runs right to left), short status words only where status is not normal, and a sticky totals row in base currency; a statement-of-account pane (balance due, credit, ageing, open items with a ruled total); a sectioned, tabbed form with a money field and currency chip, the base-currency equivalent, dd/mm/yyyy dates, inline validation and a full-width sticky save bar; users and roles with seat figures, access changes and a permission matrix.

**Why better.** Than A, B and C: numbers become the design rather than content inside boxes; the amount hierarchy, lighter decimals, ruled totals and inline trend, credit and ageing charts let a manager read the state of a customer or the business without opening a report. Than Dynamics 365: no grey chrome, no ribbon to scan, a statement-style pane instead of a factbox of labels, and Arabic set in a proper Naskh and Plex pairing. Than Odoo: amounts aligned and typed for reading, the currency beside every figure, red reserved for what is late, and the morning briefing that says what needs doing before showing figures. Best fit for owners, finance managers and credit controllers who judge the system by how clearly it shows money; the cost is slightly fewer rows than A at the default density.
