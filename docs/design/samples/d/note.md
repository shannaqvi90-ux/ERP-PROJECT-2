# Direction D: "Command centre"

Dark-first, built for the person who lives in the system all day. The keyboard is the hero: a command field sits in the top bar and on the home page, Ctrl K opens a command palette (shown in `screenshots/command-palette.*.png`) that finds records, runs actions on them and jumps anywhere; every button shows its shortcut, and an IDE-style status bar keeps the keys in sight. Screens are split views: a crisp, high-information grid beside a record pane, so nothing opens on top of the list. Light and high-contrast themes use the same tokens.

**Palette.** Dark ground #08070D with three surface steps (#0F0E17, #14131E, #1A1926) and hairlines #22202F, so depth comes from tone, not borders. One accent, the brand's dark violet #9B5CFF (#6A00E8 in the light theme). Glow (`--glow`, `--glow-soft`) is used only on the command field, the primary action, the active tab, the selected palette item and the focused field. Status colours are quiet: mint, amber, coral, blue, each AA on its surface. Overdue amounts are amber, negatives coral in brackets. High contrast is black with white rules, yellow focus and no glow.

**Type.** English: Geist (variable, SIL OFL) for the interface, Geist Mono (SIL OFL) for codes, TRNs, references, dates in lists and keys. Arabic: Readex Pro (variable, SIL OFL), a clean geometric Arabic designed for screens; it is second in the same stack, so names, TRNs and emails share one baseline in mixed lines. Scale 10.5/11/12/13/14/16/20/26/30 px; Arabic is one step larger with 1.7 leading for running text, 1.5 inside dense cards and grids, and no letter-spacing. Tabular figures on every amount; the decimals are lighter in headline figures; codes keep a fixed 12 px so a 15-digit TRN always fits.

**Spacing and shape.** 4 px grid. Radius 4 to 12 px. Compact is the default (30 px rows, 28 px controls); Comfortable gives 40 px rows. The switch is always in the top bar.

**Components.** Icon rail with labels and a glow edge on the open module; top bar with both wordmarks, breadcrumb, command field (Ctrl K), density, theme and language; status bar with sync state, company, base currency and the keys for the screen. Home: actions first (overdue to chase, approvals, stock to reorder, credit breached), each card with its count, total, top three items and one action key; then five key figures with 12-month area sparklines and deltas; then recent activity and an empty-state card that leads to the first step. Contacts: query-style filter tokens, a grid with status dot, TRN, credit-used micro-bar, overdue, balance and a 12-month sparkline, and a record pane with balance, overdue and credit tiles, ageing bar, open items and actions with keys. Form: record header with key figures, tabs, a numbered section index with error and change dots, three-column sections, money fields with a currency chip and the base-currency equivalent, dd/mm/yyyy dates, inline validation, an inspector (credit, ageing, who created and changed the record), and a floating save bar that counts unsaved changes and errors. Settings: users grid, a role pane with a permission matrix, access changes this month, sign-in policy and seats. Sign-in: the palette idea shown beside a plain, fast form with passkey and company sign-on.

**Why better.** Than A, B and C: A is fast but looks like every business tool; B is calm but shows less; C is beautiful but slower to scan. D shows more per screen than A (status, credit used, overdue and trend in one row) and makes every action reachable without the mouse. Than Dynamics 365: no ribbon to hunt through, no page reloads between list and record, a real dark theme, and Arabic set in a designed Arabic face rather than a fallback. Than Odoo: a split view instead of switching between list and form, a command palette that acts on records (not only searches menus), ageing and credit visible in the list, 3-decimal KWD, BHD and OMR, and a true right-to-left layout with mirrored charts. Best fit for accountants, credit controllers and operations staff who work from the keyboard all day; the cost is a more technical feel that some owners may find less warm than C or F.
